# Audio — Help Me Breathe

Everything in `audio/` is generated by `tools/make-soundscapes.mjs`, sample by
sample, from a seeded pseudo-random generator and a handful of one-pole filters.
Nothing is downloaded, nothing is recorded, no sample library is involved and no
text-to-speech or AI music service is called. The pack is our own work and ships
as CC0.

- **The beds:** `rain`, `ocean`, `brown-noise`, `drone`, `forest`, `night` —
  60-second seamless loops, 44.1 kHz mono, encoded to Opus in Ogg (`.ogg`) and
  AAC in MP4 (`.m4a`).
- **The cue tones:** `cue-inhale`, `cue-exhale`, `cue-hold` — one-shots, same two
  formats, for optional use. The live timer still synthesises its cues in Web
  Audio; these files exist for the video renderer and for any surface that cannot
  run an oscillator.
- **The index:** `audio/manifest.json`.
- **The player:** `js/pro/soundscapes.js`.

## Why the product is voice-free

This is a deliberate product decision, not a gap.

Hosted text-to-speech services commonly restrict their free tier to personal or
evaluation use and put commercial rights behind a paid plan; some also restrict
using the output inside a product that is itself sold. That is the general shape
of the market, not a citation — no provider's terms were read for this document,
and none is named. Anyone who wants to reopen the question should read a specific
provider's terms on the day and record what they say.

The reason it matters here is the direction of the risk. Ambient soundscapes are
a **paid** feature — they are part of the Pro unlock. Shipping free-tier TTS
audio inside a paid feature would put any licensing problem at the centre of the
flagship paid product, and it would survive in every copy a customer ever
downloaded for offline use. Synthesis costs nothing and removes the question
instead of answering it.

Recording a real voice has the same shape of problem in a different place: it
needs a performer, a release, a re-record every time the copy changes, and a
separate recording per language.

Voice-free costs nothing, needs no recurring subscription, has no licence
attached to it, and is what most people actually want under a breathing pacer.
The pacer itself already carries the instruction — the circle, the text and the
cue tone all say *inhale* without anyone saying it out loud.

The same reasoning applies to the beds. A CC0 field recording still needs
provenance checking, and "CC0" on an aggregator site is an assertion by an
uploader, not a warranty. Synthesising the beds removes the question entirely:
there is no third party anywhere in the chain.

## How each bed is made

All six are built from three kinds of layer, mixed and then normalised to a
target RMS with a peak ceiling of 0.94.

| Bed | Recipe |
|---|---|
| `rain` | White noise highpassed at 1.1 kHz and lowpassed twice at 7.5 kHz for the hiss, pink noise lowpassed at 900 Hz for the body, a 20-second breathing LFO on the hiss level, and about three droplets per second — damped sines between 1.7 and 4.3 kHz with 8–25 ms decays. |
| `ocean` | Pink noise lowpassed at 620 Hz as the body, white noise banded 1.4–6 kHz as foam. A ten-second swell drives the body between 34 % and 100 %, and drives the foam by the same curve raised to the power 2.4 so the foam only really arrives at the crest. A 30-second drift modulates the depth of the swell. |
| `brown-noise` | A leaky integrator (6 dB/octave), highpassed at 22 Hz to kill the DC wander and lowpassed at 2.6 kHz. Nothing moves. |
| `drone` | Six sines at 55, 55.05, 82.5, 82.62, 110 and 110.08 Hz, each with a slow gain LFO. The small offsets beat against each other, which is what makes it move without an LFO doing the work. A little pink air on top, then a circular lowpass at 1.8 kHz. |
| `forest` | Pink noise banded 180 Hz–2.4 kHz as the floor, white noise banded 2.5–9 kHz as leaves, both under a 15-second wind swell. Then one to three note "songs" every three seconds or so — short frequency sweeps between 2.3 and 4.6 kHz — and a lower call every twenty seconds. |
| `night` | Brown noise lowpassed at 1.4 kHz with a trace of pink air, kept very quiet (0.055 RMS, the quietest bed in the pack), plus a low pulse every twelve seconds: 48, 96 and 144 Hz under a slow raised-cosine swell. |

### Making a 60-second loop seamless

Three tricks, one per kind of layer.

1. **Noise layers** are rendered longer than the loop — the loop, plus a
   two-second crossfade, plus a one-second warm-up that the filters eat and that
   is then discarded. The tail is equal-power crossfaded into the head, so the
   last sample flows into the first with no click and no filter start-up
   transient.
2. **Tonal layers and every LFO** use frequencies snapped to an integer multiple
   of 1/60 Hz, so they complete a whole number of cycles across the loop and are
   periodic by construction. 55 Hz is 3300 cycles per minute; 55.05 Hz is 3303.
3. **One-shot transients** (droplets, chirps) are written with a modulo index, so
   an event scheduled near the end wraps around into the beginning.

A filter applied *after* those layers are mixed would undo all of it — a one-pole
starts from zero state, so its first samples carry a transient its last samples
do not. That is exactly what happened to the drone during development: a
seam 74× larger than the typical sample-to-sample step, which is an audible tick
once a minute. `circularFilter()` runs the filter over two back-to-back copies of
the buffer and keeps the second, so the state entering sample 0 is the state
leaving sample n−1. The seam dropped to well under 1× the typical step.

### Verifying a build

The measurement worth repeating after any change: render with `--keep-wav`, then
compare `|s[0] − s[n−1]|` against the mean absolute difference between adjacent
samples. A ratio near or below 1 is inaudible. Anything in the tens is a click.

Do the same on the **encoded** files, not only the WAVs, because the codec puts
some of the seam back. Decoding the shipped pack and measuring the same ratio at
the 60-second mark gives:

| Bed | `.ogg` (Opus) | `.m4a` (AAC) |
|---|---|---|
| `rain` | 0.1× | 0.6× |
| `ocean` | 1.1× | 0.3× |
| `brown-noise` | 0.7× | 0.3× |
| `drone` | 7.6× | 59.7× |
| `forest` | 0.1× | 0.4× |
| `night` | 0.6× | 3.1× |

Five of the six survive encoding intact. `drone` does not, and it is the reason
the player crossfades rather than trusting `loop = true`: it is close to a pure
bass tone, so its mean sample-to-sample step is tiny and the codec's ringing at
the file edges is large next to it. On AAC that is a step of roughly 0.045 in a
signal whose peak is around 0.1 — an audible tick once a minute if the buffer
were looped raw.

## Regenerating the pack

```sh
cd <repo root>
node tools/make-soundscapes.mjs
```

That writes `audio/<name>.ogg`, `audio/<name>.m4a` and `audio/manifest.json`, and
deletes the intermediate WAVs. A full build takes about a minute.

```
--only=rain,ocean   rebuild only these beds (cues are always rebuilt)
--seconds=60        loop length
--bitrate=48        target kbps for the beds
--keep-wav          keep the intermediate 44.1 kHz WAVs for inspection
--out=audio         output directory, relative to the repo root
--ffmpeg=PATH       use a specific ffmpeg binary
--help
```

Output is deterministic where it matters: the seed for each bed is derived from
its name, so the same script produces byte-identical WAVs every time, and the
AAC files come out byte-identical too. The Ogg files do **not** — an Ogg stream
carries a randomly chosen serial number, so `.ogg` bytes differ on every run even
though the audio is identical. Sizes stay the same to the byte; a rebuild that
changes a size has changed the audio.

`--only` merges into the existing manifest rather than replacing it, so a partial
rebuild does not drop the beds it did not touch.

### ffmpeg

The script needs an ffmpeg that can encode **both** Opus and AAC. It searches, in
order: `--ffmpeg=`, `$HMB_FFMPEG`, `ffmpeg` on `PATH`, the Playwright bundles
under `%LOCALAPPDATA%/ms-playwright/ffmpeg-*/`, the `imageio_ffmpeg` binaries
that ship inside a Python site-packages tree, WinGet packages, and the usual
Unix locations. Every candidate is probed with `ffmpeg -encoders` before it is
used.

That probe matters. **The Playwright ffmpeg cannot do this job.** It is built
`--disable-everything` with only VP8, PNG and WebM enabled — no audio encoders at
all, not even PCM. The build on this machine came from `imageio_ffmpeg`
(a gyan.dev essentials build, `--enable-libopus`), found automatically at:

```
%LOCALAPPDATA%/Python/pythoncore-3.14-64/Lib/site-packages/imageio_ffmpeg/binaries/ffmpeg-win-x86_64-v7.1.exe
```

If no usable ffmpeg is found the script still runs, but it degrades: 30-second
beds written as 22.05 kHz WAV and full-rate WAV cue tones, with a warning on
every affected file. That output is far too large to commit — it exists so a
contributor without ffmpeg can still hear what they changed, not so it can ship.
Install ffmpeg and re-run before committing.

### Sizes

Bitrate steps down automatically (48 → 40 → 32 → 24 kbps) until a file fits under
400 KB, which is the ceiling FEAT-12 of the private build spec
(`docs/private/BUILD_SPEC.json`, gitignored, not a public document) asks for.
The current build:

| | Opus (`.ogg`) | AAC (`.m4a`) |
|---|---|---|
| `rain` | 313 KB | 365 KB |
| `ocean` | 307 KB | 365 KB |
| `brown-noise` | 321 KB | 365 KB |
| `drone` | 322 KB | 366 KB |
| `forest` | 309 KB | 365 KB |
| `night` | 324 KB | 365 KB |
| cue tones (3) | 13 KB total | 24 KB total |

**4.03 MB for the whole pack**, against an 8 MB budget. Ambient noise is close to
the worst case for a perceptual codec — there is no tonal structure to throw
away — so these files do not compress the way speech or music would at the same
bitrate.

## How the site plays them

`js/pro/soundscapes.js` exports
`initSoundscapes(rootEl, { requirePro, track })`. `js/pro/index.js` imports it
dynamically inside a `try/catch`, so a missing or broken audio module never takes
the breathing timer down with it.

- **One page-wide player.** It borrows the engine's `AudioContext` from
  `js/app.js` and connects to `audioSystem.masterGain`, so a bed sits *underneath*
  the inhale and exhale cue tones rather than beside them, and one context serves
  the page however many timers it carries. `masterGain` already sits at 0.3, so
  the bed's own gain compensates for that.
- **Looping.** The beds are seamless end to end, but Opus and AAC both add
  encoder padding that a plain `loop = true` can expose as a tick. The player
  schedules overlapping copies one period ahead — the buffer minus the crossfade
  — each fading in and out on a 1.5-second equal-power curve, and falls back to
  `loop = true` if the browser refuses a value curve.
- **Format.** Opus first where `canPlayType` says it plays, AAC otherwise, and
  decoding retries down the list, because `canPlayType` describes the media
  element and `decodeAudioData` is a separate code path in some browsers.
- **Gestures.** Nothing starts until the page has seen a real `pointerdown`,
  `keydown` or `touchend`. `data-autostart` alone is not a gesture and will not
  start a bed.
- **Sessions.** `hmb:session-start` starts the armed bed; `hmb:session-stop` and
  `hmb:session-complete` fade it out and leave it armed for next time.
- **Tab visibility.** Hidden tab, faded out; visible again, back on. Background
  timer throttling would otherwise starve the loop scheduler.
- **Free tier.** One 20-second preview per bed per page load, spent the moment it
  starts. After that, `requirePro('soundscapes')` — and a free visitor who has
  spent a bed is not asked again at the top of every session. A preview never
  arms the bed for a later page load: `hmb.soundscape` only records `on: true`
  for a paying visitor, so nobody is met by audio they did not ask for, and the
  paywall card does not reappear at the end of every future session. The bed
  they chose and the volume they set are still remembered.
- **Crisis-safe pages.** On `<body data-no-asks="true">` a free visitor gets no
  Soundscapes button at all, so those pages carry no ask. A paying visitor keeps
  the feature.
- **Offline.** As soon as the entitlement says `pro` (a trial or a
  subscription), and on every later page load, the player copies
  `audio/manifest.json` and one file per bed (the format this browser will
  actually decode, so about 1.9 MB) into a Cache named `hmb-audio`, one file at
  a time in the background. Reads check that cache before the network. That is
  what FEAT-12 means by precaching the pack for subscribers only: someone who
  starts a trial on the train and then loses signal has the pack already,
  rather than only the beds they happened to play while online. It is skipped
  when the browser reports `navigator.onLine === false` or
  `connection.saveData`, and files already present are skipped, so the re-check
  costs nothing. **Nothing at all is cached here for a free visitor**, which is
  what keeps the free-tier cache exactly as small as it is today.

  `sw.js` keeps `hmb-audio` across shell-cache bumps: its `activate` handler
  deletes every cache whose name is not in `KEEP_CACHES`, and that set holds
  both the current `CACHE_NAME` and `hmb-audio`, so a deploy no longer wipes a
  customer's offline pack. The per-load re-check only fills gaps: a browser may
  still evict the cache under storage pressure, and a bed that is missing is
  fetched again the next time the customer is online.
- **Storage.** Choice, volume and whether the bed rides sessions live in
  `localStorage` under `hmb.soundscape`, written through `js/storage.js` like
  every other key on the site.
- **Degrading.** No Web Audio, no `fetch`, no manifest or no decoder: the button
  simply does not appear and the timer is unchanged.

Memory: a decoded 60-second bed is roughly 11 MB of `Float32` samples, so the
player keeps at most two decoded buffers and evicts the oldest.

That string is in `audio/manifest.json`, once at the top level and once on every
entry. The factual half is verifiable: every sample in the pack was computed by
`tools/make-soundscapes.mjs` from a seeded PRNG and arithmetic. There is no
sampled material, no recording, no AI-generated audio, no third-party asset and
no attribution requirement anywhere in it.

The **CC0 half is a choice, not a fact**, and it is waiting on the owner. CC0
waives our rights and lets anyone — a competitor included — reuse the beds.
"Generated by Help Me Breathe, all rights reserved" is the alternative and costs
nothing to switch to: change the `LICENSE` constant in
`tools/make-soundscapes.mjs` and re-run, which rewrites the manifest. Because
that decision is still open, the product UI does **not** state a licence; the
Soundscapes panel says only what the audio is ("generated by us from scratch: no
recordings, no samples, no voice"). Keep it that way until the owner has chosen.

If CC0 stands, a subscriber redistributing the audio creates no obligation for us
and none for them.

## Known limits

- The beds are **mono**. Stereo would roughly double the size for an ambience
  that is meant to sit under a voice-free pacer, and mono behaves better on a
  single phone speaker.
- Loops are **60 seconds**. A ten-minute session hears the same minute ten times.
  The crossfade hides the seam, but a listener paying attention to the forest
  will recognise the same bird. Longer loops trade linearly against file size.
- The cue tone files are **not wired into the live timer**. It still synthesises
  its cues with an oscillator, which is smaller, sample-accurate and follows the
  technique's own frequencies. The files exist for `render.html` and for anything
  that cannot run Web Audio.
- Encoder padding is worked around, not removed. If a future browser reports
  gapless Opus reliably, the crossfade scheduler could be dropped in favour of
  `loopStart`/`loopEnd`.
- The crossfade is not free. Overlapping the tail with the head sums two copies
  of the bed that are 58.5 seconds out of phase, so the level wanders a little
  across the 1.5 seconds it lasts. Measured on the shipped files in 50 ms
  windows, the worst case is about −3 dB on `drone` and `night` and −2 dB on
  `rain` — a slow, shallow dip once a minute, well inside the natural swell of
  the beds themselves, and much cheaper than the tick it prevents. Worth
  re-measuring if a future bed is more tonal than `drone`.
- Nobody has listened to these on studio monitors. They were verified
  numerically — seam continuity, RMS, peak, DC offset, decoded duration — not by
  ear on a range of devices.
