0. Read this part first

Most data processing addendums exist because a supplier holds a great deal of personal data on a customer's behalf. This one exists for the opposite reason: to write down, in a form you can file, that Help Me Breathe does not receive your clients' personal data at all.

The Help Me Breathe timer, the embedded widget and the client session links all run inside the browser of whoever opened them. There is no account system, no client record, no session database and no server that receives what anybody practised. So for the data you are most likely to be worried about — who your clients are, what they did, when and how often — there is no processing to describe, because none of it reaches us.

Help Me Breathe is also not a company. It is one person, Georges Rayess, resident in Lebanon, trading under that name. There is no legal entity, no data protection officer, no compliance department and no audited security programme. This document is written to be accurate about that rather than to look like something larger. If your organisation needs a supplier with SOC 2, a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, ISO 27001 or a completed vendor questionnaire, this addendum will not satisfy it and you should not buy the licence.

1. The parties

The Controller is you: the individual practitioner, teacher, coach, school staff member or facilitator who bought the licence, named and signed below. You decide who your clients are, what you tell them, and what records you keep about them.

The Provider is Georges Rayess, an individual trading as Help Me Breathe, resident in Lebanon, contactable at contact@helpmebreath.com.

"Personal data", "processing", "controller", "processor", "data subject" and "personal data breach" have the meanings given to them in the UK GDPR and in Regulation (EU) 2016/679. Where your own law uses different words for the same ideas, read them across.

2. What this addendum is attached to

This addendum forms part of the Help Me Breathe terms of service for the duration of your Practitioner or Studio licence. It adds to those terms; it does not replace them. Where a clause here and a clause in the terms of service genuinely cannot both be true, this addendum wins on questions of personal data and the terms of service win on everything else.

3. Roles

You are the controller of any personal data relating to your clients. The Provider is a processor only in respect of the narrow set of data described in clause 5, and is a controller in its own right for its own business records — its invoices, its email correspondence with you, and its licence administration.

In respect of your clients' personal data, the Provider is neither a controller nor a processor, because it does not receive that data. Clause 6 sets out how that works technically, and the privacy attestation states it as a signed, dated statement.

5. The only personal data that flows anywhere

There are exactly three flows, and they all concern you rather than your clients.

  • Your licence email at the merchant of record. When you buy, our merchant of record takes the payment as the seller on record and holds your email address, your payment details and the country and tax information it needs to charge the right amount. The Provider sees your email address and the transaction record. The Provider never sees your card number.
  • Your licence key and token. A licence key is issued against that email address. When a browser activates it, that browser sends the key to the licence endpoint and receives a signed token, which is stored in that browser's own local storage. The token records a tier and an expiry date. It does not record who you are or what you practised.
  • Email you send. If you write to contact@helpmebreath.com, the Provider has your message and your address, in an ordinary email mailbox, for as long as that correspondence is useful.

6. What is not processed, and why

None of your clients' personal data is transmitted to, stored by, or accessible to the Provider. In detail:

  • There is no client account, no client profile and no client identifier. Nothing on the site asks for a client's name, email address, date of birth or health information.
  • The timer runs entirely in the browser. Session settings, session history and streaks are written to that browser's own local storage under the hmb. prefix and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • A co-branded client session link carries its whole configuration inside the link itself — the technique, the length, your name, your message. Opening it writes nothing to any server, and no record exists of which link was created, sent, or opened. It does load the Quicksand typeface from Google Fonts, so Google's font servers see the reader's IP address and user agent in the ordinary way, and a practitioner logo, where the link carries one, is fetched by the reader's browser from the practitioner's own server. Neither request carries anything about the session. The embedded widget under /embed/v1/ makes no third-party request at all, so use it instead where that matters.
  • The embedded widget makes no third-party requests of any kind: no fonts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking pixels load inside the frame.
  • Class and projector mode, and the printable handouts, are entirely local. Practitioner details typed into the handout page are stored in that browser under hmb.practitioner and are never sent to the Provider.

There is one unavoidable exception, and it is stated plainly rather than buried: like every website, the pages are served over the internet, so the hosting provider's servers necessarily see the requesting IP address, the user agent string and the URL requested in order to return the page. That is ordinary web serving, it is described in clause 7, and — together with the font and logo requests noted above — it is the whole of what leaves a client's browser.

8. International transfers

The Provider is resident in Lebanon. Lebanon does not benefit from a European Commission adequacy decision. Two things follow, and both are stated openly.

First, no client personal data is transferred anywhere, because none of it is collected — so for the data that matters most in a therapeutic or educational setting, the transfer question does not arise.

Second, the limited licence-administration data in clause 5 does reach Lebanon, because that is where the Provider reads email. Where you are established in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland and require a transfer mechanism for that data, the Provider will sign the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (Module Two, controller to processor) and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum on request, before you buy. Please ask first rather than after; the Provider will not claim to have safeguards already in place that have not been executed.

9. The Provider's obligations

  • Documented instructions. The Provider processes the clause 5 data only to run the licence and answer your email, and on your documented instructions, unless required otherwise by a law that applies to it. If the Provider believes an instruction breaks data protection law, it will say so rather than follow it quietly.
  • Confidentiality. The Provider keeps your data confidential. There are no employees; where a contractor is ever engaged, they are bound to the same duty in writing before they get access.
  • Security. The Provider maintains the technical and organisational measures described in clause 10 and in the security overview.
  • No selling. Your data is never sold, rented, shared for cross-context behavioural advertising, or used to train any model.
  • Assistance. The Provider will help you, so far as it reasonably can and at no charge for a request of ordinary size, to answer a data subject request, carry out a data protection impact assessment, or consult a supervisory authority.

10. Security measures

The measures below are the ones actually in place. The security overview describes them at more length and is treated as an annex to this addendum.

  • The site is static. There is no application database and therefore no store of personal data to breach.
  • All traffic is served over TLS, with HTTP requests redirected to HTTPS by the host.
  • Secrets — the licence signing key and provider API keys — live only in the hosting platform's encrypted environment variables. They are not in the source repository and are not written to logs.
  • Licence keys are never written to logs, analytics or error reports in full.
  • Licence tokens are signed with HMAC-SHA256 and carry a short expiry, so a tampered or stale token simply fails and the browser falls back to the free tier.
  • Serverless functions apply best-effort per-address rate limiting.
  • Security headers are set at the edge: content type sniffing off, a restrictive referrer policy, a permissions policy denying camera, microphone and geolocation, and framing denied everywhere except the widget and client-link paths that are meant to be embedded.
  • The Provider's own accounts with the hosting platform, the merchant of record and the email provider use unique passwords stored in a password manager and two-factor authentication.

11. Personal data breach

If the Provider becomes aware of a personal data breach affecting your data, it will notify you by email, to the address on your licence, without undue delay and in any case within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. The notification will describe what happened, what data was involved, what the likely consequences are, and what is being done about it — including the parts that are not yet known, said as such rather than guessed at.

Because the Provider holds no client data, a breach of the Provider's systems cannot expose your clients' details. A breach at the merchant of record could expose the billing information you gave them; they notify the Provider under their own terms, and the Provider will pass that notification on to you.

12. Data subject requests

If you receive a request from one of your clients about the timer, the honest answer is usually short: no personal data about them exists to access, correct, export or erase, because none was collected. If it helps, the Provider will confirm that in writing to you, referencing the privacy attestation.

If a data subject contacts the Provider directly, the Provider will not respond substantively to a request that concerns your data. It will tell them to contact you and let you know it happened.

For your own data, write to contact@helpmebreath.com and you will get a reply within two business days.

13. Audit and information

The Provider will answer reasonable written questions about this addendum and provide the documents on this page, within two business days. What the Provider cannot offer is an on-site audit, a penetration test report, a SOC 2 report, an ISO certificate, or a completed vendor security questionnaire, because a one-person operation has none of those things. If your policy requires them, please treat that as a reason not to buy rather than something to be negotiated afterwards.

14. Return and deletion

On request, or within 30 days of your licence ending, the Provider will delete the personal data it holds about you, except records it must keep for tax and accounting law and any email correspondence that is evidence of an unresolved dispute. Records held by the merchant of record are governed by their own retention policy, which is theirs rather than the Provider's to set.

Your clients' data needs no deletion step, because it was never collected. Data in a client's own browser is deleted by that person clearing their browser storage, and the Provider cannot reach it in order to delete it for them.

15. Term, changes and law

This addendum starts on the effective date above and runs until your licence ends and the retention periods in clause 14 have passed.

If this addendum changes materially, the Provider will email licence holders at least 30 days before the change takes effect. A copy of the version you signed remains the version that governs your licence unless you agree in writing to a newer one.

The Provider's total liability under this addendum is subject to the limit in the terms of service. Nothing in this addendum limits either party's liability for anything that cannot lawfully be limited, or affects a data subject's rights against either party under applicable data protection law. Governing law and jurisdiction are those stated in the terms of service.
