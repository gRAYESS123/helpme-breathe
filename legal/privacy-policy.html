Privacy Policy

Georges Rayess — writes and maintains Help Me Breathe. Not a clinician.

Last updated 11 September 2026. Published by Georges Rayess, an individual.

Help Me Breathe is a guided breathing timer at helpmebreath.com. It is published by one person — Georges Rayess, an individual resident in Lebanon — and not by a company. The timer works without an account. When you subscribe you get an account, and this policy says exactly what it holds.

This policy explains what the site keeps on your device, what leaves your device and when, who else is involved, and what you can ask me to do. It is written to be read, not to be survived. If anything here is unclear, write to contact@helpmebreath.com and I will answer in plain words.

The short version

  • The timer runs entirely in your browser. Your settings, your session history and your saved patterns stay on your device.
  • You can use the timer without an account. An account exists only if you subscribe, and it holds your email address and your plan — never your practice.
  • There are no passwords. You sign in with an email link or a Google account.
  • Analytics and advertising cookies are set to denied before anything loads, and stay denied unless you choose "Accept all" in the cookie banner.
  • Payments are handled end to end by a merchant of record. I never see your card details, and I do not store them anywhere.
  • One free trial per person. Checking that uses a one-way hash of your email address and a random identifier in a cookie on this device. No browser fingerprinting.
  • I do not sell personal information and I do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

1. Who is responsible for your data

The controller for this site is Georges Rayess, an individual, contactable at contact@helpmebreath.com. There is no data protection officer, because a one-person project does not need one and I would rather not pretend otherwise.

Two other parties are controllers in their own right for the parts they handle: the merchant of record that sells the subscription, and Google, for the analytics and advertising services described below. Each is described in the section that covers it.

2. What is stored in your browser

The timer keeps a small amount of information in your browser's local storage, under keys that all begin with hmb. This is storage on your own machine. It is not a cookie, it is not sent with your requests, and I cannot read it.

What Help Me Breathe stores in your browser
What Why it exists
Settings Your chosen technique, session length, sound and vibration toggles, so the timer opens the way you left it.
Session history A short list of finished sessions — date, technique, seconds, breath count, whether you finished. Capped at the most recent 500. It powers the streak view and the CSV export.
Consent choice Whether you chose "Accept all" or "Essential only" in the cookie banner, so you are not asked on every page.
Saved presets Custom breathing patterns you build and name yourself, in the Pro pattern builder.
Small flags Whether you have acknowledged a safety note, whether night mode is on, and whether a prompt has already been shown once.

All of it is written and read by code running on your device, and all of it stays there. None of it is transmitted to me, to Google, or to anyone else. The two cookies described in section 6 are separate from this, and they are listed there. You can wipe every bit of it by clearing site data for this domain in your browser, and the site will work exactly the same afterwards — it will simply have forgotten your preferences.

If your browser blocks storage — a private window, a locked-down profile, a full quota — the timer keeps the same values in memory for that page's lifetime instead, and forgets them on reload. Nothing breaks.

3. What leaves your device, and when

3.1 Loading a page

Like every website, asking for a page means your browser sends a request. The host that serves this site receives your IP address, the page requested, your user-agent string and the referring page, and keeps short-lived operational logs for security and troubleshooting. This is ordinary web hosting, not a profile of you.

Pages on this site also load the Newsreader and IBM Plex Sans typefaces from Google Fonts. That request reveals your IP address to Google. The embedded widget and the co-branded client session links deliberately do not, as explained in section 4.

3.2 Analytics — Google Analytics 4 with Consent Mode

I use Google Analytics 4 to see which pages help people and which do not. It runs with Google Consent Mode v2, and every consent signal starts at "denied" — analytics storage, advertising storage, advertising user data and advertising personalisation are all refused before any Google script has a chance to run.

If you choose "Essential only" in the cookie banner, or ignore it, they stay denied. No analytics cookie is written. If you choose "Accept all", analytics is enabled and Google Analytics records page views, the technique you selected, whether a session started or completed, and similar interaction events. IP anonymisation is on. Events never carry an email address, an account identifier or anything you typed.

Google puts it this way: Your web browser automatically sends certain information to Google. This includes the URL of the page you're visiting and your IP address. Those sentences are from Google's page on how it uses information from sites that use its services. You can change your decision at any time by clearing this site's storage, which brings the banner back.

3.3 Advertising — Google AdSense on content pages only

Some of the free written guides carry advertising from Google AdSense. This pays for the hosting. The rules I hold myself to are narrow and worth stating:

  • Ads appear on content and comparison pages only, below the fold. Never inside the breathing viewport.
  • There are no ads at all on the anxiety and panic-attack pages, on the product pages, on the embedded widget, or on this page.
  • Ads are hidden while a breathing session is running.
  • Anyone with an active subscription sees no advertising anywhere on the site. The ad loader checks before it loads, and stops if you have one.
  • If you did not accept advertising cookies, the loader requests non-personalised ads instead.

Google's own explanation is that Cookies help to make advertising more effective. You do not have to accept that trade. To opt out of personalised advertising across Google's products, use Google's advertising page, which states that You can use ad settings to manage the Google ads you see and turn off personalized ads. The settings hub itself is at myadcenter.google.com, and the choice you make there follows you across sites, not just this one. Declining advertising cookies in our banner, or holding an active subscription, also works.

3.4 Your account, and what it holds

You do not need an account to use the timer. An account exists only when you subscribe, and an account is for one person or one household.

You sign in with an email link or a Google account. There is no password, so there is no password to store, to leak or to reuse somewhere else. Signing in with Google tells Google that you signed in here and tells this site the email address and the account identifier Google returns; it does not give this site access to your Google data.

The account holds exactly this, and nothing else:

  • your email address;
  • the sign-in identities you have used — the email link, the Google account, or both;
  • the date the account was created;
  • your plan, its status and its period dates — when the current period started and when it ends;
  • the payment provider's subscription identifier and customer identifier, so a receipt, a cancellation or a refund can be matched to the right account.

What the account does not hold is as important: no card number, no expiry date, no security code, no billing address, no session history, no streak, no saved pattern and nothing about what you practised or when. Those stay in your browser, as section 2 describes. No card details ever reach us at all.

3.5 Subscribing — the merchant of record

I do not sell to you directly and I never touch your payment details. The subscription is sold through a merchant of record, which is the seller of record for the transaction: the contract of sale is between you and it. It takes the payment, calculates and remits sales tax and VAT, issues the receipt, and is the controller of your payment data in its own right. It then pays me. The service itself is provided by Georges Rayess.

That provider is being appointed. It will be named on this page, and on the security overview, before checkout opens, and its own privacy notice will govern your payment data. The company named as seller on your receipt is the merchant of record for your purchase.

What reaches me is limited to what section 3.4 lists: your email address, your plan and its dates, and the provider's subscription and customer identifiers. I never receive your card number.

3.6 The free-trial check

One free trial per person. When you start a free trial we check whether this email address has already had one. To do that we store a one-way hash of the email address — we cannot turn it back into an email address — and a random identifier in a cookie on this device. That is the whole mechanism.

There is no browser fingerprinting. Nothing reads your fonts, your screen size, your canvas, your audio stack, your installed plugins or any other signal your browser gives away, and nothing builds a device profile. If you clear the cookie, the cookie is gone; the email hash is what makes the rule hold.

The hash and the random identifier are kept for 24 months from the last activity on that trial record, and section 9 explains why that period is what it is.

3.7 The email list

You can ask for occasional email — a note when a new technique or guide goes up. It is entirely optional and nothing on the site requires it.

It works on double opt-in. You enter your address, the provider sends you a confirmation email, and nothing is sent until you click the link in it. If you never click, the address is left unconfirmed and receives nothing. The list is run by our email service provider, a third-party mailing platform that stores the address and the confirmation record on my behalf as a processor. Only a twelve-character hash prefix of an address ever appears in this site's own logs.

Every email carries an unsubscribe link, one click, no questions and no retention email. You can also write to contact@helpmebreath.com and ask to be removed. When you leave, the address is deleted from the list.

3.8 Writing to me

If you email me, I have your address and whatever you wrote, for as long as the conversation is useful and my records need to exist. I read that email in Lebanon. Support correspondence is not added to the mailing list.

4. The embedded widget

The breathing widget that other sites embed is deliberately sealed. Inside the frame there are no cookies, no analytics and no advertising — no web font, no tracking pixel, no consent script, no measurement of any kind. The frame loads its stylesheet and the timer code from this domain and nothing else, with two narrow exceptions that are worth naming rather than glossing over:

  • White-label verification. If an embed carries a subscriber's token, the frame asks this site whether that token is still valid, so it knows whether to drop the attribution. The request goes to helpmebreath.com, carries the token and nothing about the visitor, and is not recorded against anyone.
  • A practitioner's own logo. A white-label embed can show the practitioner's logo. If they host that image somewhere other than their own site, the visitor's browser fetches it from that host, which sees the visitor's IP address the way any image on any page does. That is the only outbound request the frame can ever make, the practitioner chooses the address, and it cannot happen on the free embed at all.

Beyond those two, a visitor to a site that embeds the timer is not tracked by me in any way.

The widget may keep a plain aggregate, cookieless count of how many times it has loaded, so I know whether the feature is used at all. Such a count records no identifier, no IP address and nothing about any individual visitor, and it cannot be linked back to a person or a session. No per-visitor measurement will ever be added to the frame.

5. Practitioners and their clients

If you have an active subscription and use the timer with your own clients, classes or students, I process no data about those people. Their sessions run in their own browsers and stay there. There is no client record, no attendance log, no completion report and no database in which such a thing could exist.

That means you are the controller of your own client relationship and of anything you choose to record about it. I am not a processor of your client data, because I never receive it. What I do hold for you is the narrow account record in section 3.4: your email address, your plan and its dates, and the payment provider's identifiers. A signable data processing addendum, a dated privacy attestation and a security overview covering all of this are published in the compliance pack.

6. Cookies and similar technologies

Your settings and your session history live in local storage, described in section 2, which is not a cookie and is not transmitted. Beyond that, this site sets two cookies of its own, and only in connection with a subscription. Both are first-party, both carry the __Host- prefix, and neither is used for advertising or measurement.

Choosing "Essential only" means neither Google cookie is set, and the timer behaves identically. Nothing on this site is degraded, delayed or nagged because you declined.

7. Affiliate links

A small number of pages link to products I think are genuinely worth the money — currently Headspace and moonbird. Those are affiliate links, marked as sponsored, and if you buy after clicking one I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Clicking such a link takes you to that company's own site, where its own privacy policy and its own cookies apply, and it may set a cookie to attribute the sale. Nothing about you is sent to them by me. If you do not click, no data is shared. The recommendation on those pages is never for sale — the comparison page still concludes that the free option is enough for most people.

8. Legal bases for processing

For readers in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, the bases I rely on are:

  • Consent — analytics and advertising cookies, and the optional email list. Withdrawable at any time, and withdrawing is as easy as giving it. Nothing the subscription needs in order to work relies on consent, and nothing is refused to you for declining.
  • Performance of a contract — your account, your subscription, the payment, and supporting you while it runs.
  • Legitimate interests — the one-way hash of an email address in the free-trial ledger and the random identifier in the __Host-hmb_did cookie, both so that the free trial stays one per person and the offer is not drained by repeat sign-ups; keeping the site up; and answering your email. These interests are weighed against your rights, and the trial check is deliberately the least a rule like that can be built on — a hash and a random number, with no fingerprinting and no profile.
  • Legal obligation — tax and accounting records, which the merchant of record largely holds.

9. How long things are kept

Browser storage lives on your device until you clear it, and nothing expires it on a schedule. The rest has a number:

  • Account data — kept for as long as the account exists, and for 30 days after it is deleted, so a deletion done by mistake can be undone and a final receipt can be matched.
  • Free-trial records — the one-way email hash and the device identifier are kept for 24 months from the last activity on that record. The reason is plain: otherwise deleting an account would reset the free-trial limit.
  • The __Host-hmb_did cookie — 2 years. The __Host-hmb_ent cookie — 14 days.
  • Mailing list entries — kept until you unsubscribe.
  • Purchase and tax records — kept by the merchant of record for as long as its own law requires.
  • Server logs — short-lived and operational. Email correspondence — kept while it is useful, then deleted.

10. Your rights, and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access the personal data held about you, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or object to its processing, to receive it in a portable form, and to withdraw consent. Under the GDPR and the UK GDPR those rights are yours as a matter of law.

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the CPRA, California residents may also ask what personal information is collected, ask for it to be deleted or corrected, and opt out of sale or sharing. I do not sell personal information and I do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of there. You will never be treated differently for exercising a right.

How to exercise any of them, including deleting your account or getting a copy of it: email contact@helpmebreath.com and say what you want. There is no self-service button for this yet — the account pages are still being built — so email us and it is done by hand. I will answer within thirty days, and sooner if it is simple. Write from the address on the account, or quote the order reference on your receipt, so I can be sure I am answering the right person. For anything touching a payment, ask the merchant of record on your receipt as well — it holds the payment record, not me. If you are unhappy with my answer, you may complain to your national data protection authority.

One honest limitation: most of what this site holds about you is not held by me at all. It is in your browser, where you can read it and delete it yourself in seconds.

11. Children

This site is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The timer itself needs no personal information from anyone. If you believe a child has sent me an email address or opened an account, write to contact@helpmebreath.com and I will delete it.

12. International transfers

This is a small site with a global audience, so data crosses borders. Pages are served from a content delivery network with locations worldwide. The merchant of record, the mailing platform and Google each operate their own international infrastructure and their own transfer safeguards, described in their own policies. The narrow set of account and subscription data reaches Lebanon, because that is where I read email; Lebanon has not been the subject of a European Commission adequacy decision. Where you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland and need a transfer mechanism for that, ask before you buy and I will sign the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum. I will not claim to have safeguards in place that have not actually been signed.

13. Security

The site is served over HTTPS. Secrets live in server environment variables, never in the code and never in a browser. There are no passwords to store, because sign-in is an email link or a Google account. Email addresses in the free-trial ledger are stored only as a one-way hash, email addresses in logs only as a short hash prefix, and no payment data reaches this project at all. The account record is deliberately small, so a breach of it would expose an email address and a plan, not a practice history and not a card. No system is perfectly secure, and I will not promise that it is.

14. Changes to this policy

I will update this page when the facts change — a new provider, a new feature, a different ad partner. The date at the top always says when it last changed. If a change is material, I will say so clearly on the page rather than quietly editing a sentence. Continuing to use the site after a change means the updated policy applies.
