4-7-8 Breathing Technique: Step-by-Step Guide to Better Sleep

The 4-7-8 breathing technique is a counted pattern popularised by Dr Andrew Weil: in for four, hold for seven, out for eight. People use it as a wind-down cue at bedtime and as something specific to count when they feel wound up. What none of this shows is that a breathing timer treats insomnia, anxiety or any medical condition.

How the 4-7-8 Breathing Technique Works

The usual explanation is that a slow, long out-breath shifts the balance towards the parasympathetic branch of the nervous system — the "rest and digest" side. That is the mechanism people reach for, and it is plausible, but it is a proposed explanation rather than a demonstrated one: the studies that exist mostly measure how people say they feel, not what their nervous system did.

What people use it for

These are reasons people give for practising it, not effects the pattern has been shown to produce. Read them that way.

  • A wind-down cue before sleep: many people use it as the last thing they do before lights out, because it marks the end of the day with something other than a screen.
  • Something specific to count: a number to follow is easier for some people than being told to relax.
  • A longer exhale than you would settle into on your own: the eight-count out-breath is deliberately longer than the in-breath.
  • A short, portable thing to do under stress: it needs no equipment and nobody around you has to know you are doing it.

For what the evidence does and does not support — with sample sizes, the authors' own wording and the limits written down next to the findings — see what the research says. The short version is that the trials are small, mostly unblinded, and measure how people feel rather than whether anything is treated.

Before you use the seven-count hold

4-7-8 holds the breath for seven seconds every round, so this section is not boilerplate.

  • Talk to a clinician first if you are pregnant, or if you live with a cardiovascular condition, uncontrolled high blood pressure, epilepsy, a respiratory condition such as asthma or COPD, or glaucoma, or if you have had surgery recently.
  • If you live with panic attacks or panic disorder, holding the breath can feel like the beginning of one. Cyclic sighing gives you the long out-breath without any hold.
  • Practice sitting or lying down. Never while driving, cycling, swimming or in a bath.
  • Stop if you feel dizzy, light-headed, tingly or short of breath, and breathe normally until it passes. That feeling is a reason to shorten the pattern, not to push through it.
  • This is a relaxation exercise. It is not treatment for insomnia, anxiety or anything else. See our medical disclaimer.

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1: Get Comfortable

Find a quiet space and sit or lie down comfortably. Place the tip of your tongue against the tissue ridge behind your upper front teeth throughout the exercise.

Step 2: Exhale Completely (Preparation)

Exhale completely through your mouth, making a "whoosh" sound. This prepares your lungs for the breathing pattern.

Step 3: Inhale for 4 Seconds

Close your mouth and inhale quietly through your nose while mentally counting to 4. Keep the inhale gentle and controlled.

Step 4: Hold for 7 Seconds

Hold your breath while mentally counting to 7. This is where the magic happens – your body begins to relax.

Step 5: Exhale for 8 Seconds

Exhale completely through your mouth while making the "whoosh" sound and counting to 8. This extended exhale activates relaxation.

Step 6: Repeat

Complete 3-4 cycles when starting. As you become more comfortable, you can work up to 8 complete cycles.

Tips for Success

  • Start slowly: Begin with 3-4 cycles and gradually increase
  • Practice regularly: Try to practice at the same time each day
  • Don't force it: If you feel dizzy, return to normal breathing
  • Be patient: Benefits increase with consistent practice over time
  • Create a ritual: Combine with other relaxation practices

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 4-7-8 breathing technique safe during pregnancy?

While breathing exercises are generally safe, pregnant women should consult their healthcare provider before starting any new breathing practice, including the 4-7-8 technique.

How long does it take for the 4-7-8 technique to work?

Some people notice they feel calmer after a single round; others feel nothing much the first few times. We cannot tell you how long it takes to change your sleep, because we have not read a study of this pattern that measured that. Treat it as a habit you are trying rather than a course of treatment with an expected result.

Can I practice 4-7-8 breathing multiple times per day?

Yes, you can practice 4-7-8 breathing 2-3 times daily. It's especially effective before bedtime, during stressful moments, or when you need to calm your mind.

What the research actually says

The 4-7-8 pattern comes out of pranayama practice rather than out of a trial. The few studies of this specific count are small and short-term; we quote them, with their limits, on the 4-7-8 timer page. That is why there is no list of proven benefits on this page.

What does exist is a small body of work on slow breathing in general: a handful of randomised trials, most of them unblinded, most of them measuring self-reported mood or sleep rather than a clinical outcome. We keep every one we have read on a single page, with the sample size, the sentence from the paper that supports the claim, and the limits written down beside it. If you want to check the reasoning rather than take our word for it, that is where to go: what the research says.
